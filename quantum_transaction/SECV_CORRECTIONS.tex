\section*{C1 \quad Sec.~V-C: $\beta_z$ range and effective sample size}

These two numbers are the minima over the \emph{40-instance sweep} of
Sec.~V-E. They are quoted in the paragraph describing the single campus
snapshot, where they do not hold.

\Was
\begin{quote}
The local sampling exponents $\beta_z$ range from $0.38$ to the common
target of $1.50$ and are reweighted before coordination. At least $88$ of
the $200$ samples remain effective in every zone after reweighting,
indicating that the local sample sets maintain substantial statistical
support for the subsequent boundary decisions.
\end{quote}

\Now
\begin{quote}
The local sampling exponents $\beta_z$ range from $1.36$ to the common
target of $1.50$ and are reweighted before coordination. At least $198$ of
the $200$ samples remain effective in every zone after reweighting,
indicating that the local sample sets maintain substantial statistical
support for the subsequent boundary decisions.
\end{quote}

\Why for the snapshot instance ($g=5$, seed $0$), $\beta_z\in[1.36,1.50]$
and $\min_z\widetilde K_z=198$ of $200$
(\texttt{python eval\_numbers.py}). The values $0.377$ and $87.7$ are the
minima over all zones of all 40 sweep instances
(\texttt{scaling\_data.npz}, columns \texttt{beta\_min}, \texttt{ess\_min}).

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Note.} The sweep-wide statement is worth keeping,
but it belongs in the Setup and must say so. The tracked
\texttt{manuscript\_260901.tex} places it there already and writes
``Across zones, $\beta_z$ ranges from $0.38$ to $1.50$ \ldots stays at or
above $88$ of the $200$ retained'' --- correct for the whole evaluation,
though ``across zones'' should be read as across every zone of every
instance, which is worth making explicit.

\section*{C3 \quad Sec.~V-C: peer range and the excluded seeds}

No instance reached the centralized optimum; $100.0\%$ is a rounding of
$99.97\%$ that reads as if one had. The three dropped seeds are also
unstated.

\Was
\begin{quote}
Across 13 instances on the same map, the achieved utility ranges from
$97.3\%$ to $100.0\%$, with a mean of $99.2\%$.
\end{quote}

\Now
\begin{quote}
The instance is drawn from a family of 16 seeds on the same map, 13 of
which are globally feasible. Across those 13, the achieved utility ranges
from $97.3\%$ to $99.97\%$, with a mean of $99.2\%$.

\Why $97.34$--$99.97\%$, mean $99.19\%$, over the 13 feasible of 16 seeds
(\texttt{python eval\_numbers.py}).

\emph{is} $96.79$--$100\%$: the sweep contains an instance at exactly

\section*{C4 \quad Sec.~V-E: the partition budget is met, not undercut}

The partitioner enforces $\mathrm{n2q}(z)\le\mathrm{budget}$, and one
instance lands on $2000$ exactly, so ``below'' is not literally true.

\Was
\begin{quote}
\ldots the largest zone circuit grows from $1{,}693$ to $1{,}950$ gates, a
factor of only $1.15$, and remains below the 2000-gate partition budget for
every region size.
\end{quote}

\Now
\begin{quote}
\ldots the largest zone circuit grows from $1{,}693$ to $1{,}950$ gates, a
factor of only $1.15$, and never exceeds the 2000-gate partition budget at
any region size.
\end{quote}

\Why the plotted curve is the per-size mean of each instance's largest
zone, $1693\rightarrow1950$ ($\times1.152$), which the text reports
correctly; but the worst single zone anywhere in the sweep is $2000$, in
one of the 40 instances (\texttt{scaling\_data.npz}, column
\texttt{zone2q}; constraint at \texttt{model\_partition.py:119}).

\medskip\noindent Same subsection, the dropped instances should be stated:

\Was
\begin{quote}
The sampled region is varied over seven sizes with several instances at
each size, giving 40 instances in total.
\end{quote}

\Now
\begin{quote}
The sampled region is varied over seven sizes with six seeds each; two of
the 42 instances are globally infeasible and dropped, leaving 40.
\end{quote}

\Why \texttt{scaling\_data.npz} stores \texttt{skipped}~$=2$.

\section*{C5 \quad Sec.~V-A: the ninefold reduction is a bound, not the measurement}

$(2k+1)^2=9$ is the $\mu_z\to0$ limit. The zones of this evaluation are not
in that limit.

\Was
\begin{quote}
For small $\mu_z$, \eqref{eq:success} gives $P_z(k)\simeq(2k+1)^2\mu_z$,
giving approximately a ninefold reduction in the sampling cost for $k=1$.
\end{quote}

\Now
\begin{quote}
For small $\mu_z$, \eqref{eq:success} gives $P_z(k)\simeq(2k+1)^2\mu_z$, so
$(2k+1)^2=9$ bounds the reduction in sampling cost at $k=1$; the ratio
$P_z(k)/\mu_z$ measured over the zones of this evaluation is $5.9$.
\end{quote}

\Why $P_z(k)/\mu_z=5.9$ at $k=1$ (\texttt{python eval\_numbers.py}).

\section*{C6 \quad Appendix~C: the worked decimation step, and its FIXME}

Every number in this paragraph checks out. The commitment order does not:
the run fixes UE~$12$ first, and UE~$14$ ends on RB35, not RB52. The
\verb|% FIXME| comment that follows the paragraph must be deleted with the
edit.

\Was
\begin{quote}
\ldots When the consensus fixes UE $14$ to RB52, filtering by
\eqref{eq:app-filter} retains the $0.63$ branch, the recomputed marginal of
UE $12$ drops RB52 to zero, and the next commitment selects RB38: the
infeasible pair favored by the one-pass product is thereby excluded from
commitment.
\end{quote}

\Now
\begin{quote}
\ldots Decimation commits UE $12$ first, at confidence $0.89$, to RB38.
Filtering by \eqref{eq:app-filter} then retains only the draws in which UE
$12$ holds RB38, so the pair (RB52,~RB52) favored by the one-pass product
leaves the zone's report before UE $14$ is considered. UE $14$ is committed
later, at confidence $0.53$, to RB35, and the excluded pair never enters a
commitment.
\end{quote}

\Why decimation trace of the snapshot run: step~$7$, UE~$12\rightarrow$
index~$1=$~RB38, confidence $0.885$; step~$19$, UE~$14\rightarrow$
index~$1=$~RB35, confidence $0.533$; final assignment UE~$12\rightarrow$
RB38, UE~$14\rightarrow$ RB35.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Verified unchanged} in the same paragraph, so the
preceding sentences need no edit:
%
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item candidate sets $\{$RB52,~RB38$\}$ and $\{$RB52,~RB35$\}$, sharing
        RB52 under $W_r=1$;
  \item the three feasible joint probabilities $0.63$, $0.31$, $0.06$
        (measured $0.629$, $0.307$, $0.065$);
  \item marginals of RB52: $0.31$ for UE~$12$, $0.63$ for UE~$14$
        (measured $0.307$, $0.629$);
  \item $19\%$ of the product's belief on the excluded pair (measured
        $19.3\%$; the second pair, zone Z0 UEs $30$~\&~$38$, also $19\%$).
\end{itemize}

\medskip\noindent Delete with the edit:
\begin{quote}\ttfamily\footnotesize
\% FIXME: verify against run log -- candidate sets of UEs 12/14, the three
joint probabilities (0.63/0.31/0.06 read off (c1)), and whether UE 14 is
indeed fixed first and to RB52. If the actual order differs, adjust the
last sentence only; the logic is unchanged.
\end{quote}
\noindent The FIXME's own instruction was right: the logic is unchanged and
only the last sentence moves.
